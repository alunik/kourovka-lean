# A semiabelian nonmonomial group of order 2592

[Problem and Lean entry points](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/README.md)

This gives a negative answer to M. Kida's conjecture, problem 21.68 of the
Kourovka Notebook. The proof below is implemented by
[`not_notebookStatement`](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Solution.lean).

## The complement and its quaternion subgroup

On the ordered quaternion basis `(1,i,j,k)`, let `E` be the diagonal sign
changes whose four signs have product `+1`. Thus `E` is elementary abelian
of order eight. The even permutations of the four basis elements form
`T ≅ A₄ ≅ C₂² ⋊ C₃`. Set

$$
W=E\rtimes T,\qquad |W|=96.
$$

Left multiplication by the quaternion units gives signed permutations

$$
L_i:(1,i,j,k)\mapsto(i,-1,k,-j),\qquad
L_j:(1,i,j,k)\mapsto(j,-k,-1,i).
$$

Both permutations are even and both have two negative signs, so they belong
to `W`. They generate a quaternion group `Q` of order eight. Let `s` fix `1`
and cycle `i,j,k`. It is an unsigned even permutation of order three and
normalizes `Q`. Consequently

$$
H=Q\rtimes\langle s\rangle\le W,\qquad |H|=24,\qquad[W:H]=4.
$$

The Lean construction defines `T` directly as `C₂² ⋊ C₃`, with its affine
action on four points, and checks the signed-permutation embedding. No
SmallGroups catalogue lookup enters the proof.

There is no subgroup of index two in `H`. Such a subgroup would be the
kernel of a nontrivial homomorphism to `C₂`. Every such homomorphism kills
`s`, and the conjugation cycle forces the images of `i,j,k` to be equal.
The relation `ij=k` forces that common image to be the identity. Since these
elements generate `H`, the homomorphism is trivial.

## A representation of degree two

Over the complex numbers, put

$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm i&0\\0&-\mathrm i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
R=PQ,\qquad U=-\tfrac12(1+P+Q+R).
$$

These satisfy the quaternion relations, together with
`U³=1`, `UP=QU`, and `UQ=RU`. Thus they define a representation `ψ` of `H`
by sending `i,j,s` to `P,Q,U`, respectively. It is irreducible: the only
`P`-invariant complex lines are the coordinate axes, and `Q` interchanges
those axes. The formal proof instead verifies the exact character norm one
using a 24-term integer calculation; the preceding matrix identities are
checked first over Gaussian integers.

## The abelian kernel and its inertia subgroup

Let `X=W/H` be the four left cosets. With the usual permutation action of `W`,
take the augmentation module

$$
A=\left\{a\in\mathbb F_3^X:\sum_{x\in X}a_x=0\right\},\qquad |A|=27,
$$

and form `G=A⋊W`. Its order is `27·96=2592`. The embedded chain

$$
1<C_3<T<W<G
$$

exhibits semiabelianity: its successive abelian semidirect-product kernels
are `C₃`, `C₂²`, `E` and `A`.

Choose a primitive cube root of unity `ω`. Each coordinate gives a linear
character `λ_x(a)=ω^{a_x}` of the additive group `A`. These four characters
are distinct: if `x≠y`, the augmentation vector `e_x-e_y` has values `1` and
`−1` in those coordinates, and `ω≠ω⁻¹`.

Put `λ=λ_H`. The action on the coordinate characters is the coset action, so
the stabilizer of `λ` in `W` is exactly `H`. Its inertia subgroup in `G` is

$$
I=I_G(\lambda)=A\rtimes H.
$$

The formula `λ̃(a,h)=λ(a)` defines a linear character of `I`. Let
`ρ=λ̃·Inf_H^I ψ`; scalar twisting and inflation preserve irreducibility,
so `ρ` is irreducible of degree two. Define

$$
\chi=\operatorname{Ind}_I^G\rho.
$$

Its degree is eight. Mackey's irreducibility criterion applies: for any
`g∉I`, the intersection `I∩gIg⁻¹` contains `A`, on which the two relevant
representations have different scalar characters. An intertwiner between
them must therefore vanish. The identity double coset contributes the
one-dimensional endomorphism space of `ρ`. Hence `χ` is irreducible.

## The obstruction to linear induction

Suppose `χ=Ind_L^G θ` with `θ` linear. Comparing degrees gives `[G:L]=8`.
Since `A` is a normal subgroup of order 27,

$$
[A:A\cap L]=[AL:L]\mid[G:L]=8.
$$

Thus `A≤L`. This argument uses normality and coprime orders; `A` is not a
Sylow subgroup of `G`.

Frobenius reciprocity supplies a nonzero `θ`-eigenvector in the restriction
of `χ` to `L`. The restriction to `A` is the direct sum of the four
distinct weights `λ_x`, each with multiplicity two. Therefore `θ|_A=λ_x`
for some `x`. A linear character is invariant under conjugation by its
own group, so `L` lies in the inertia subgroup of `λ_x`, a conjugate of `I`.
That inertia subgroup has index four in `G`. Projecting to its quotient by
`A` produces a subgroup of `H` with index `8/4=2`, a contradiction.

Therefore `G` is semiabelian and has an irreducible nonmonomial complex
representation. This disproves the complete conjecture.

## Provenance

Kida already records the relevant order-96 semiabelian group containing
`SL₂(3)` in *On semiabelian groups*, Journal of Group Theory 28 (2025),
697–712, p. 710: [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1515/jgth-2024-0010),
[open text](https://d-nb.info/1364524341/34). The complement/subgroup pair is
