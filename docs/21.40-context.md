# Origin and context of Problem 21.40

[Problem and proof](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_40/README.md) · [Documentation](README.md)

Checked against primary sources on 15 September 2026.

## Earliest exact formulation verified

The question is attributed to **A. Dantas and E. de Melo** in
[arXiv:1401.0300v39](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v39), dated
**8 January 2026**, Problem **21.40**, printed page **167**. This was
the initial arXiv version of the twenty-first edition. The editor's
[announcement followed on 9 January](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/2026/01/09/the-new-21-st-edition-of-kourovka-notebook/).
The preceding [v38](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v38), dated 3 November 2025,
was still the twentieth edition. No reference to an earlier paper accompanies
21.40; the adjacent Apps reference belongs to 21.39.

The formulation asks whether a subgroup of `GL_n(ℚ)` with finitely many
orbits under its abstract automorphism group must be virtually soluble.
It appears on page 173 in the later
[1 September 2026 revision, v46](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v46).
September is therefore a revision date, not the earliest verified version
containing this problem. The arXiv identifier's original 2014 date concerns
the evolving Notebook, not 21.40.

No earlier exact formulation was located in the accessible papers and
theses checked. This identifies the earliest verified public source in
this investigation, not the date of original conception or proof that the
Notebook was its first publication anywhere. Appropriate wording is
**“the question of Dantas and de Melo, recorded as Problem 21.40”**.

## The preceding research programme

Write `ω(G)` for the number of full abstract automorphism orbits on `G`.
The programme seeks to determine how much group structure follows from
`ω(G) < ∞`.

| --- | --- | --- |
| [Bastos–Dantas–de Melo, 2019 preprint / 2020 publication](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.01375v1), Theorem A, p. 2 | A soluble group of finite rank with finite `ω(G)` splits as a torsion-free radicable nilpotent subgroup by a finite subgroup | Assumes solubility and finite rank. Its subsequent question about removing finite rank is a different problem |
| [Bastos–Dantas–de Melo, 2020 preprint / 2021 publication](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10800v1), Theorem 1.2, p. 2; Corollary 1.3, p. 3 | Structural decompositions for virtually nilpotent groups, and for virtually soluble groups of finite rank | Virtual nilpotence or solubility is a hypothesis |
| [Kato, 2025 PhD thesis](https://repositorio.unb.br/handle/10482/53504), pp. 77–78 | Nearby questions about nilpotency class and characteristic subgroups of `UT_n(ℚ)`, plus discussion of linear groups, Tits' alternative, and Mal'cev's theorem | Evidence of the preceding programme; its Questions 3.1–3.6 do not state 21.40 |
| [de Melo–Kato, October 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09353v1), introduction, p. 2; Theorems 1.1–1.2, p. 3 | Explicitly motivates classifying linear groups with finite `ω(G)`; determines the finite/infinite orbit threshold for full unitriangular groups | A concrete family and broader motivation, without the exact virtual-solubility question |
| [Dantas–de Sousa, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.02090v1), Theorems A–C, pp. 2–3 | Residually finite groups, finitely generated locally graded groups, and rational completions of free nilpotent groups | Later related progress; these hypotheses do not cover arbitrary rational linear subgroups |

The first two papers appeared in *Geometriae Dedicata* **209** (2020),
119–123, and *Archiv der Mathematik* **116** (2021), 261–270.
Their arXiv submission dates are 4 August 2019 and 25 August 2020.
The unitriangular preprint was submitted on 10 October 2025 and the
locally graded preprint on 3 May 2026.

## What the solution contributes

Rational linearity and finite orbit count now imply virtual nilpotence
directly. No solubility, nilpotence, finite rank, or finite generation is
assumed. The quantitative theorem gives a normal subgroup of index at
most `(2n+1)^(n²)`, one rational unitriangular basis, torsion-freeness, and
a nilpotency-class bound. Restriction of scalars extends the finite-index
torsion-free nilpotent conclusion to number fields.

The infinitely generated case matters: `(ℚ,+)` embeds in `UT_2(ℚ)`, has
two automorphism orbits, and has no nontrivial finite quotient. Thus
residual-finiteness arguments for finitely generated linear groups do not
settle this question. The 2026 finitely generated locally graded result
already implies finiteness for the finitely generated linear case.

The classification inside the nilpotent family remains substantial.
For example, de Melo–Kato prove finite automorphism-orbit counts for
`UT_n(ℚ)` when `n ≤ 5` and infinitely many when `n ≥ 6`. Torsion-free
nilpotence alone therefore does not imply the finite-orbit hypothesis.
The new theorem also does not establish an absolute bound on nilpotency
class independent of the matrix dimension.

## Scope of the provenance check

The accessible sources checked include both Bastos–Dantas–de Melo
preprints in full, the relevant text and theorem statements of the
unitriangular preprint, the locally graded preprint, and Kato's 2022
master's thesis and 2025 doctoral thesis. The doctoral thesis was defended
on 21 July 2025 and made available in the institutional repository on
22 December 2025; those are distinct dates.

One access limit remains: the full text of Dantas–de Melo–Kato,
[*On soluble groups of finite rank with finitely many automorphism orbits*](https://doi.org/10.1080/00927872.2025.2578695),
*Communications in Algebra* **54** (2026), 2341–2347, was unavailable.
The publisher records online publication on 14 November 2025 and an
abstract concerning soluble finite-rank groups with four or five orbits.
That abstract cannot establish whether the article also mentions 21.40.
The thesis with the same title is a separate document. We therefore do
not claim that this paper contains, or does not contain, the exact question.
