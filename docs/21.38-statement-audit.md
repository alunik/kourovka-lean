## Statements and concrete witness

[Statement.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Statement.lean) credits Donoven and
Harper's original [Question 2](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.05498v2#S5), asking
for an infinite group of spread one, before the unrestricted Notebook question.
The source question and the formal quantifiers agree.

The closed declarations in
[Solution.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Solution.lean) are:

- `Kourovka.P21_38.originalQuestion : OriginalQuestion`;
- `Kourovka.P21_38.notebookStatement : NotebookStatement`.

The first supplies a concrete `G : Type`, a `Group G`, `Infinite G`, and
`HasSpreadExactlyOne G`. The second follows by discarding infinitude. Both
use the actual subtype `Kourovka.P21_38.diagonalSubgroup` as their witness.

[EndpointSlopes.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Proof/EndpointSlopes.lean)
defines `F` as the subtype of `GroupApproximation.HigmanThompson.compactF 0 1`.
These are actual increasing permutations of `ℚ`: the permutation and its
inverse are grid-affine with slopes integral powers of two, and the
permutation fixes every rational outside `(0,1)`. The diagonal subgroup
requires equality of the two integer endpoint germ exponents, whose
existence, uniqueness, and group laws are proved. The construction uses
concrete arithmetic and permutation operations; generation is subsequently
proved, rather than included among model-defining hypotheses.

The formal existence result concerns this rational PL group. A separate
formal identification with a group of real PL homeomorphisms, or with real
derivative definitions of endpoint slopes, is not asserted by this audit
and is not needed for either existential statement.

## Ordinary spread and all input elements

[Spread.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Proof/Spread.lean) defines
`GeneratesPair x y` by `Subgroup.closure ({x, y} : Set G) = ⊤`, with closure
taken inside `G`. `HasSpreadAtLeast G k` quantifies over every `Fin k → G`
whose entries are nonidentity. There is no restriction to conjugate
companions, normal generation, or a topological closure.

The expanded conclusion checked in Lean is:

```text
∃ G : Type, ∃ group : Group G,
  Infinite G ∧
  (∀ x : G, x ≠ 1 → ∃ y : G, ⟨x,y⟩ = G) ∧
  ∃ a b : G, a ≠ 1 ∧ b ≠ 1 ∧
    ∀ y : G, ¬ (⟨a,y⟩ = G ∧ ⟨b,y⟩ = G).
```

Here each displayed `⟨x,y⟩` means ordinary subgroup closure as above. The
bad pair is chosen before quantifying over the companion. The theorem
`Kourovka.P21_38.diagonal_spread_profile` additionally proves that the tuple
condition holds precisely for `k ≤ 1`.

[PrescribedGeneration.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Proof/PrescribedGeneration.lean)
proves `exists_generating_companion` for every nonidentity element of the
diagonal subgroup. Its `GenerationData` is constructed from the sole
nonidentity assumption. In particular, no nonzero endpoint-exponent
assumption excludes elements of the endpoint kernel. An independent Lean
test explicitly specialized this theorem to an arbitrary nonidentity
`f : F` with `f.1 ∈ compactCore 0`; it compiled successfully.

The generation chain supplies actual subgroup elements through branch
communication, finite interpolation, endpoint cancellation, and
commutator extraction. Proved perfectness then gives the entire compact
core, and powers of the companion fill the diagonal endpoint quotient.
No caller-supplied generation or interpolation hypothesis remains in the
closed existence theorems.

[UpperBound.lean](../Kourovka/Problems/P21_38/Proof/UpperBound.lean) proves
`diagonal_exists_pair_no_common_companion` using concrete nonidentity core
elements and their fixed sets. Its `diagonal_infinite` proves infinitude
by an injective sequence of powers of an actual element moving `1/2`.
