# How the proof works: Kourovka 21.68

This informal account was generated by **Nilradical v0** after human acceptance of the linked proof snapshot. **Agent-generated exposition; not refereed.** It may simplify details. Kida asks whether every finite semiabelian group is monomial. The accepted answer is negative: a group of order 2592 has an irreducible complex representation of degree eight that cannot be induced from any linear character of any subgroup. The construction and formalization are credited to Nilradical v0, with prior mathematical input and reused Lean infrastructure identified below. No minimal-order claim is made.

## What the statement requires

A semiabelian group comes with an increasing chain of actual subgroups from the identity to the whole group. Each next subgroup must be a quotient of an abelian semidirect product with its predecessor. The definition [IsSemiabelian](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Statement.lean#L27) records the chain, its endpoints, monotonicity and the surjections witnessing its steps.

There is a source distinction worth keeping visible. The Notebook's literal formulation does not explicitly require the auxiliary abelian factors to be finite, while Kida's Definition 2.1 does. The Lean step follows the former wording. This witness also satisfies the finite-factor requirement: its chain uses the concrete finite groups C₃, C₂², E and A. A general equivalence between these formulations is not an additional formalized endpoint of this result. The [independent statement audit](../nilradical-v0-verification/statement-audits/21.68.md) explains that boundary.

Monomiality has its usual representation-theoretic meaning in [IsMonomialRepresentation](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Statement.lean#L33): an isomorphism to induction of a subgroup character with values in ℂˣ. Categorical simplicity is connected to ordinary nonzero irreducibility by [FDRep.simple_iff_isIrreducible](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/External/TauCeti/RepresentationTheory/Simple/Basic.lean#L189). The universal [NotebookStatement](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Statement.lean#L43) quantifies over all finite groups.

## Building the group around an obstruction

Begin with H = Q₈ ⋊ C₃, where the element of order three cycles the quaternion units i, j and k. This group has order 24 and no subgroup of index two. Indeed, a quotient map to C₂ kills the element of order three; conjugation then makes the images of i, j and k equal. Since ij = k, that common image must be trivial. Lean's [H_no_index_two](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/Groups.lean#L77) implements this through subgroup membership and an explicit generator normal form.

Embed H into W = E ⋊ T. Here T = C₂² ⋊ C₃ acts on four points, and E consists of the sign changes on those points with an even number of minus signs. Thus E has order eight, T has order twelve and W has order 96. Left multiplication by quaternion units gives the required signed permutations; their embedding is proved injective. Kida's *On semiabelian groups*, page 710, already records the relevant order-96 semiabelian group containing SL₂(3). This complement/subgroup pair is prior input, as documented in the [problem references](../../Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/README.md#references-and-credits).

Let X = W/H, a four-element coset space. Take A to be the functions X → 𝔽₃ whose coordinates sum to zero. There are three independent coordinates, so A has order 27. Permuting coordinates gives an action of W; the counterexample is G = A ⋊ W. The theorem [card_G](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/AbelianKernel.lean#L183) certifies 27 · 96 = 2592.

The embedded chain 1 < C₃ < T < W < G establishes semiabelianity. Each step adds an abelian split extension. Lean does not replace the chain definition with an assumed closure property: [IsSemiabelian.append](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/Semiabelian.lean#L19) transports an existing chain along an embedding and appends a step. Repeated application yields [G_semiabelian](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/AbelianKernel.lean#L121).

## From four coordinates to degree eight

Choose a primitive cube root of unity ω. Each coordinate x of A supplies the character λₓ(a) = ω raised to aₓ. These four characters remain distinct on the augmentation submodule: for different coordinates, the vector with entries 1 and −1 separates them. Their permutation action is precisely the action on X. Consequently the character λ at the identity coset has inertia subgroup I = A ⋊ H, of index four in G.

In the source, the embedded copy of A is called N, its character is `nWeight`, and [nWeight_inertia](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/AbelianKernel.lean#L250) identifies the exact inertia subgroup. This equality matters: a mere containment would not justify either the irreducibility argument or the later index calculation.

The group H has an explicit two-dimensional complex representation ψ. Its quaternion generators act by the matrices P = diag(ⅈ, −ⅈ) and Q with rows (0, 1), (−1, 0). The order-three generator acts by U = −(1 + P + Q + PQ)/2. The matrix identities realize the required conjugation cycle. Lean proves irreducibility through the exact character norm: [quaternionCharacterValue_norm](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/QuaternionRep.lean#L139) evaluates a sum over all 24 elements, and the following simplicity instance applies the character criterion.

The character λ extends to I by ignoring its H coordinate. Inflate ψ along I → H and multiply its action by that extended scalar character. The resulting [inertiaRep](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/InertiaRep.lean#L64) stays irreducible: inflation along a surjection and multiplication by nonzero scalars preserve invariant subspaces. Inducing it to G gives the degree-eight `witnessRepresentation`.

The scalar action of N makes Mackey's criterion particularly transparent. Outside I, conjugation changes λ. Any unwanted intertwiner would have to commute with two different scalar actions of some n in N, forcing the intertwiner to vanish. [simple_ind_of_scalar](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/ScalarInduction.lean#L50) packages this argument. The public [simplicity and dimension proofs](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Solution.lean#L26) then use the same witness.

## Why no linear induction can give this representation

Suppose the witness were induced from a linear character θ of L. Its dimension forces [G:L] = 8. Normality of N implies that [N:N ∩ L] divides both 27 and 8, so N ≤ L. This is a coprime-index argument; N is not a Sylow subgroup of G.

Frobenius reciprocity supplies a nonzero θ-eigenvector in the restricted representation. Its N-weight must be a conjugate of λ, and L preserves that weight. Hence L lies in the corresponding conjugate of I. Since that inertia subgroup has index four, L has index two inside it. Quotienting by N produces an index-two subgroup of H, contradicting the initial obstruction.

Lean makes the weight extraction concrete through evaluation in the coinduced function model. The [inducedCoordinate](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/Obstruction/InducedCoordinates.lean#L28) maps jointly detect zero, so a nonzero eigenvector has a nonzero coordinate with a definite scalar weight. This avoids choosing a coset transversal in the theorem statement. The general [not_isMonomial_ind_of_scalar](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Proof/NonmonomialInduction.lean#L20) assembles the obstruction, and [witness_not_monomial](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Solution.lean#L34) specializes it. The final [not_notebookStatement](https://github.com/alunik/kourovka-lean/blob/5a6b2c18e326b7b0281f00b64cade629acbfe1f5/Kourovka/Problems/P21_68/Solution.lean#L47) applies the universal conjecture to G and derives the contradiction.
