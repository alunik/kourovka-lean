## Exact result and frozen scope

The source is I. B. Gorshkov's Problem 21.53, inheriting the hypotheses of 21.52: a **finite nonabelian simple group**, a **whole conjugacy class of involutions**, and permutations preserving the actual integer product-order labels. The question asks whether preserving orders 2 and the least odd prime divisor $p$ always forces preservation of every product order. I reread printed p.175 of [Notebook v46](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0300v46). The [live arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300) still lists v46, submitted 1 September 2026; the [editors' repository](https://kourovkanotebookorg.wordpress.com/repository/) has no 21.53 solution entry at the final check. These status observations are supporting evidence, not a proof of openness.

The reviewed endpoint is the negative universal statement `Kourovka.Problem2153.not_statement : ¬ Statement.{0}`, with the whole-class carrier and fixed-color definitions in the trusted challenge. The frozen contract actually hashes to:

`e02b24ffc6e1c9c8adb3aa507a9c59de2df762492495f27dd8d788133de257ff`.

Its challenge hashes to `9f28dde89cf12a709007361f261ec4e050c674a03277686565f3129ef5f11381`. This report assesses the novelty of that mathematical claim; the separate semantic/integrity reviewers assess whether the proof establishes it.

The final route uses the finite group generated by Wilson's 26-dimensional matrices over 𝔽₈. It proves the needed properties directly, without making named-group recognition an assumption. In the ordinary Ree description, the class is the 2-central class of ²F₄(8). The specific permutation swaps two class elements with identical ambient centralizers and fixes every other class member. The order-three relation is empty, $p=3$, and a fixed third vertex has product orders 5 and 7 with the swapped vertices. The novelty target is this complete counterexample and its application to 21.53.
