# Proof roadmap for Problem 21.44

[Problem overview](../README.md) · [Public statement](../Statement.lean) · [Public theorem](../Solution.lean)

The witness is the subgroup generated by two compatible directed
automorphisms of the rooted five-ary tree. The proof separates full
finite-level generation from the growth estimate and joins them in
[Solution.lean](../Solution.lean). Local declarations use namespace
`Kourovka.P21_44`; the two imported modules retain `RecurrentSections`.

## The two essential bridge arguments

For density, let $P=\langle u,v\rangle$ be perfect with $u^3=v^3=1$.
The two directed lifts first produce a subdirect subgroup of $P^5$.
Conjugation on one fixed pair of coordinates shows a quotient of $P$ is
abelian, hence trivial. Two-transitivity transfers the resulting full pair
projection to every pair. The perfect-subdirect lemma recovers the entire
base group. This theorem is applied to the finite groups at every level.

For growth, an alternating word $w$ has a variation statistic $V(w)$,
counting sign changes separately along its two generator families. Each
selected five-letter block saves at least one letter among the five
sections. The greedy selection uses disjoint blocks, and the combinatorial
bound gives

$$
\sum_{i=1}^{5}\ell(g_i)+\frac{V(w)}{10}\le |w|+1.
$$

The section representatives evaluate to the actual sections in the generated
subgroup. This turns the word estimate into a bound for ordinary word
lengths. The root permutation and those sections distinguish group elements,
so their possible tuples give a valid counting bound.

## From counting to the limit

For $0<\delta<1/2$, low-variation words are bounded by
$8(n+1)e^{nH(\delta)}$. The others have total section length at most
$(1-\delta/10)n+1$. If $\gamma(k)\le C e^{sk}$ uniformly, their contribution
is bounded by

$$
60(n+2)^5 C^5
\exp\!\left(s\bigl((1-\delta/10)n+1\bigr)\right).
$$

[GrowthRate.lean](GrowthRate.lean) takes the infimum of positive rates $s$
for which such a uniform bound exists. If that infimum were positive,
a sufficiently small $\delta$ and a rate just above the infimum would make
both terms have a strictly smaller exponential rate. Polynomial factors
can be absorbed into the strict gap, giving a contradiction. The infimum
is therefore zero, which yields
$\log\gamma(n)/n\to0$ because every ball contains the identity.

This proves the required qualitative limit directly. The analytic argument
retains the additive one in the section budget and treats finite initial
segments by a multiplicative constant.

## Root convention

Lean uses `Fin 5` and

$$
(gh)_i=g_i h_{\sigma_g^{-1}(i)}.
$$

Thus the stored root permutations are $(0\,1\,2)^{-1}$ and
$(2\,3\,4)^{-1}$, with active sections at $3$ and $0$. In right-action
notation on $\{1,2,3,4,5\}$, the construction is

$$
a=(1,1,1,a,1)(1\,2\,3),\qquad
b=(b,1,1,1,1)(3\,4\,5).
$$

[Wreath.lean](Wreath.lean) fixes this convention before any section or
finite-level calculation.

## Foundations and verification

[RecurrentSections/WordGeometry.lean](RecurrentSections/WordGeometry.lean)
provides finite symmetric generating sets, ordinary word balls, and their
least-length function. [RecurrentSections/ExponentialGrowth.lean](RecurrentSections/ExponentialGrowth.lean)

initial exponential estimate. Their exact source and integration changes
are recorded in [THIRD_PARTY.md](THIRD_PARTY.md).

Lean kernel computations. All other constructions and counting arguments
are proved symbolically. The integrated endpoint has passed its target build
and axiom audit with only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`.

finite root-generation certificate. Full repository checks are recorded in the
[verification section](../README.md#verification).
