# Kourovka 21.35 — an order criterion for verbal subgroups

**Nilradical · Conditional Lean proof**

[Mathematical note](../../docs/walkthroughs/21.35.md) · [References and credits](../../docs/walkthroughs/21.35-credits.md) · [Verification record](../../docs/nilradical-21.35/README.md)

## Problem

Let $G$ be a finite group, $p$ a prime, and $w$ a multilinear commutator word. Write $G_w$ for the set of single evaluations of $w$, and $w(G)=\langle G_w\rangle$. Suppose that

$$
x,y\in G_w,\qquad p\nmid |x|,\qquad p\mid |y|
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad p\mid |xy|.
$$

Must $w(G)$ have a normal $p$-complement?

The question is due to **Yerko Contreras Rojas, Valentina Grazian and Carmine Monetta**, in [The Kourovka Notebook, 21st edition](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0300v46), Problem 21.35, printed page 172. A normal $p$-complement is a normal subgroup of order prime to $p$ and of $p$-power index.

## Result and scope

**Affirmative under the two named mathematical inputs below.** The formalization proves that the displayed condition is equivalent to the existence of a normal $p$-complement in $w(G)$. It covers every finite group, every prime and every outer commutator word. The hypothesis concerns single values; $y$ may have mixed order divisible by $p$.

The two explicit inputs are:

1. **Thompson's classification of finite minimal nonabelian simple groups**, with its five concrete families and their parameter restrictions.
2. **Coprime commutators in finite quasisimple groups:** if $H$ is finite and perfect, $H/Z(H)$ is nonabelian simple, and $\gcd(|x|,|Z(H)|)=1$, then $x$ is one commutator. This follows from Liebeck–O'Brien–Shalev–Tiep's published classification of noncommutators in quasisimple groups.

These inputs remain explicit arguments of the Lean theorem. The development proves the remaining reductions and family arguments. Ordinary Ore follows from the second input in the center-free case; Frobenius' normal-complement criterion is imported as a proved Lean theorem. Neither is a separate assumption.

## Proof outline

1. Prove the result for soluble groups by induction on the word tree, using the derived-word case and value-generation lemmas.
2. In a least counterexample, show that $G$ is perfect and its soluble radical satisfies $R=\Phi(G)=O_p(G)$, with $G/R$ nonabelian simple.
3. Use Thompson's classification and the proved family arguments to exclude $p=2$ and the odd-prime cases with noncentral radical. A surviving least counterexample must have $R\le Z(G)$.
4. Then $R=Z(G)$ is a $p$-group and $G$ is quasisimple. The coprime-commutator input makes every $p'$-element an ordinary commutator. Surjectivity of word maps on the simple quotient, together with cancellation of central factors, makes it a single value of every outer word.
5. The product-order hypothesis now forces every $p'$-element of a $p$-subgroup normalizer to centralize that subgroup. Frobenius' criterion yields a normal $p$-complement in $G$, hence in $w(G)$, a contradiction.
6. Conversely, in the quotient by a normal $p$-complement, $x$ has trivial image and $y$ has nontrivial image. The product $xy$ has the same nontrivial image as $y$, giving the required divisibility.

The [proof account](../../docs/walkthroughs/21.35.md) explains these steps and the prior mathematics they use. The substantial binary family calculations are part of the linked formal development; the short outline does not replace them.
